## design/core.md
- **Settings tokens.** Pruned, then deflated. Mode, players, nodes and seed are always emitted.
- **A seat left to the seed.** Why `random` resolves in `build_state` rather than in a dispatcher bot (the oracle contract). The pick is derived, never drawn. The pools are fixed lists, append-only (`RANDOM_POOLS`), the seed's era (seed // `POOL_ERA`) naming the one it deals from, so appending a pool re-deals no map; why the seed and not a `Settings` field; the menu's seed cap and stale builds. The log records what it dealt. The win overlay reveals it, and `Settings` keeps the placeholder.
- **Challenge links.** Score and tie rules. Links travel by clipboard only. Editing a challenge asks first rather than locking the widgets.
  - *Keys outlive the schema that made them.* `_LEGACY_KEY_DROPS`, `findTwin` folding, newest key wins, the `sc_config_key` trade-off, the int/float `aux` digest leak.
- **Ship-speed growth.** Applies at launch only, and compounds rather than growing linearly (with the reason). Re-times from `length_ly`.
- **In-lane battles.** A fight is a geometric meeting, fought pairwise; the winner is only thinned. Growth mostly switches the feature off.
- **Persistence, replay & history.** Format v2 records orders plus dice, and the alternatives that lost. Rules live in the log. The `"ai"` flag doubles as the seat claim. A resume always lands paused. A live turn's rng is derived (`replay.reseed`), so a rewind is not a re-roll. *Two streams*: bots decide in parallel from the turn's start (`engine.decide_seat`), and each fight rolls dice keyed to its place (`engine._Dice`), so seat order and unrelated moves can't move a roll; why knower seeing the real dice is fine; the `keyed` marker.
  - *`match_id`.*
- **Star names.** Generated at build time; named last so seeds don't move. Labels are placed collision-first, with a reserved slot for marks. `starlore` (generated alongside) feeds the star info card; undrawable scripts are stripped.
- **Symmetric layouts** (under "Past a standard board the box grows"). Hub, ring, wheel and core; why `hub` is pinned rather than a `RULES_VERSION` bump; added lanes chosen on one seam and rotated; why `core` opens the middle up; where `layout` is inert.

- **Rules in full** (detail kept out of `CLAUDE.md`): in-lane battles; pile-up resolution; replay and history; an all-bot game has no human seat; whole-number floats don't survive the browser; past a standard board the box grows; settings, challenge links and keys; star names.
## design/shell.md
- **Text sizing** and **`config.touch_ui`.** Measured layout. The send popup is the one place the tap floor gives way.
- **Map viewport margins.** The `node_clearance` floor, and clamping against the fit-padded span.
- **Send popup / `Ui.editing_existing`.** Unwinding to IDLE on an edit; the slider is hit-tested before the panel drag.
- **Forwarding rules.** A hold per system and a share per lane. Adding a lane splits evenly and removing one undoes it; + takes from home, then the other lanes. One rounding formula behind orders and every number shown. The popup's three shapes, and what a split sheds on a short screen. The hold badge under the system. Names fit in three steps.
- **Combat rules page.** It teaches the square law from the real code (`preview_fight`): parameters rather than `config`, corners rather than samples, demo sliders on `MenuState`, hand-broken prose, the jitter matrix.
- **Losing / spectator mode.** Keyed on defeat, plus how the reset view frames the map.
- **Ironman and the ships-lost readout.** Ironman is a local preference that hides History (and so rewind) until a game is decided (`Ui.can_review`). The top bar shows our `ships_lost`, the score's tie-break.
- **End-of-game graphs (`stats`, `render._draw_history_graph`).** History review's chart of systems, ships, income and losses per seat (per-turn losses as stacked bars, map share as 0-100% bands), docked over the map or full screen, read off the history boards; the legend toggles each trace; rivals shown only once review is revealed.
- **Naming each seat's bot (`Ui.bot_label`).** The top bar's hover tooltip and the graph legend name each seat's bot and stop; a random seat reads "Random" until the game is decided.
- **Route mode.** A proposal plus a confirm. Owned-only paths are forced. A plan can't contradict itself, but it can loop with existing rules.
  - *Two sub-modes.* Chain and rally share `flow_field`. Distance is travel turns, not hops. One Mode button. Ties split evenly. Auto-route. A tap always aims (two rejected shapes). A plan keeps a system's hold.

- **Rules in full** (detail kept out of `CLAUDE.md`): measured layout and `touch_ui`; browser bridges (`softkeyboard`, `webstore`, `share`, quitting); send popup and spectating; viewport margins; the Combat tab; route mode.

## design/gauntlet.md

  - *The stages*: the ten, and how their seeds were picked (`tools/gauntlet_check.py`, loss share 0 to 86%).
- **The file format.** For authors: `stages`/`lanes`/`start`, lanes one way unless `two_way`, every stage reachable (`gauntlet.problems`, `checkBody`), the menu's Save as a stage's `settings`, the text limits, user gauntlets (kept from links, plus the desktop folder).
- **Gauntlets made on the board.** The gauntlet page and `/api/gauntlet`: stored not carried, its own table never `campaigns`, the `<slug>-<hash>` id, one `checkBody` run twice, pruned stages, fetched once and kept (`gauntlet.remember`), the inline `#gauntlet=` form, remixing by `#<id>`, listing by an admin at the author's request.
- **Relation to the weekly campaign.** Uncontested, so no server; the graph shape and the bordering rule shared in spirit, except that a gauntlet lane runs one way (stage 6 won from a link opened 5).
- **Generated gauntlets.** A spec (stages, `fix`/`range`/`in`/`not` rules, opponents, `roll`) dealt by `dealer.deal` with opponents by depth, probed and picked by `tools/gauntlet_gen.py`; *where it runs* (dealing anywhere, measuring needs the engine, timings, web unmeasured, form on the board); *the spec*; *dealing*; *the benchmark*; *the first run* (roll off by default, depth tracks difficulty, four probes are coarse, long games, no stage won by all); *in the game* (`#gauntlet-spec=`, a slice per menu frame, config pushed back, budgets not lifted, one probe path, 18 s desktop, Copy link); *on the board* (the generate form, `gauntlet-spec.mjs`, "either" as an `in` rule); *sharing the result* (the file, not spec and seed). All four steps built; the web build untimed.
- **The gauntlet: proposed, not built.** A seeded run graph via `tools/campaign.py`'s `_layout`/`cut_nodes`, stages dealt like `_from_family`/`_mystery`, difficulty by depth through `LADDER_ORDER`.
- **Carry-over.** Boons via `custommap.from_state`, and the cost on the board; no longer blocks the run.
- **Stage targets.** An author-set score a stage must be won within before the stages beyond open (`gauntlet.Target`): gauntlet data never `Settings`, meeting it counts, it is what `gauntlet.cleared` means, a win short of it leaves the stage open, Next stage follows it (`Ui.stage_cleared`), shown on the card, top bar and list, set on the board from a pasted challenge link's score, trusted like clearing, none in the tutorial, a roster bot's score for a dealt run (not built).
  - *Beat the best bot*: the board's `standings.bestBot` on the stage's setup (found by game key or jsonb-equal `games` row), read when picked and on Save and fixed as `{turns, lost, bot, beat}`; not live, strict, wins only, no target until the setup has bot rows.
  - *Later: targets on lanes*: an optional third lane element for shortcuts, bonus stages and alternate routes; depth over ungated lanes only; reachability ignores targets.
## design/leaderboard.md
- **Bot replays.** A scheduled job rather than a service.
  - *Through `build_state`*; *what the replayed seat is tuned to* (`replay_aux`, the top of each bot's slider); *a loss is a result, not a score*; *a win stores its own replay*; *`engine_rev` hashes the simulation*; *the one table the public cannot write*; *estimated difficulty* (the share of bots that lose a map, as a sort and an Easy-to-Very-hard badge).
- **Checked scores.** The id rides on `Challenge`. Two consented senders. `game_logs` is private. No identity on a row. `is_current` and `rules_version`. The verifier binds a log to its setup, and re-rolls every keyed turn's dice (hand-picked rolls are a `mismatch`; turns from before keying pass unchecked).
  - *Watching one back* (a watched result is not ours to post); *versioning: bots are free to move, the engine is not*.
- **Playstyle readings.** The player page's panel, read by the worker from `public_replays` only, one row per match and never per name. Every field sums, so the page pools a name's rows, and the bots' column is the same shape, pooled from self-play winners (`playstyle-baseline.mjs`, generated). `rev` and `shape`. *Decided against: warming actuary's learner from a profile*.
- **The grace period.** Half an hour's grace after losing a node or its neighbour, derived in `fold`, for a game started while you still had access (stamped at Start, `scores.campaign_start`). *Decided against: a cooldown between moves; an "able to capture" flag on its own*. *In the game*: a confirm before Start and a top-bar countdown, from `/api/campaign` running the same JS.
- **Campaign fleets (proposed, not built).** Real-time lanes on the meta-map: a launch locks a claim, so a neighbour stolen mid-game no longer voids it. Holders see inbound fleets, and one fleet per player paces the week. Collisions go to the better score. Identity is the open problem.

- **Rules in full** (detail kept out of `CLAUDE.md`): the game and the board are one site; crowns, the weekly campaign and embargoes; the bot column; a replay is never shown as if it still reproduced the game.

## design/win-turns.md
- **The question.** The board ranks turns after the win, the top bots win nearly every game against the weak ones, and people beat council's score on 115 of 151 maps, mostly on turns; whether a bot can win sooner by playing closer to losing.
- **How it measures.** Cells and line-ups (one seat for every other seat, or `a+b`); every seed in every seat; fixed work; one arm's reading (won, unfinished, turns to win at p25/median/p75 with a game not won counted slower than any win; the turns to a 3:1 lead in players' income, 3:1 to 9:1 and 9:1 to the win, which every win passes, and why not the board's income or two thirds; ships lost; `low`, the lowest income share on the way); two arms paired game by game (`beats` in the board's own order, the one to gate on; Δwon; Δturns as a geometric ratio over pairs both won; the gap split by phase); the 7,000,000 seed block.
- **Ledgers and revs.** `results/win_turns/<label>/<bot>.jsonl`; `play_rev` digests the code that decides a game and `SHAPE`, the label defaults to it and a named ledger refuses another tree's rows; `--base` reads any ledger; `bot_replay.engine_rev` leaves `replay.py` (and `reseed`) out.
- **First readings (2026-10-19).** Council, Hunt and marshal against heuristic, rusherplus and thinker on six cells: Council won 1,945 of 1,950 and is fastest everywhere (beats Hunt 76.9%, 17 turns sooner); Hunt is nearly saturated; marshal is not, and 189 of its 269 games not won were unfinished at turn 600; two thirds of every bot's win, and most of each gap, comes before the 3:1 lead; the fastest bot rides no closer to the line. *Against marshal*: Council still saturated (97-100%), Hunt not (57-83%), a quarter of Hunt's gap to Council after 9:1.
- **marshal: sparing the target and feeding on every board (2026-10-19).** *What the stalemates were*: 99 of 189 blocked by the guard against the target's own stack, which no amount of waiting clears. *The two changes*: `SPARE_TARGET` (a strike on a rival spends the guard kept against that rival) and `FEED_MAX_TURNS` 0. *Measurements*: screen and fresh seeds, beats 72.3% (z +22.2), +5.8pt won, unfinished 180 to 52, 15.9% sooner; roster +8.6 against marshal, +5.9 against knower, level against actuary; guard 0 loses 8.2 to actuary; no extra cost per turn. *What else was tried*: a lead-scaled guard, no guard past an income share, a partial release, the pour floor too, pinned relief, neutral fronts feeding, no neutrals past a lead, eight constants on top. *What is left*: the land grab, choosing strikes after the lead (Council's oracle), three-seat stalls; the ladder not re-run.
- **marshal: a second round, nothing shipped (2026-10-19).** *Where marshal still loses*: three seats (behind by turn 5 in its 11:6:3 losses), Council ahead from turn 8 by taking systems a rival just emptied, rival fights failing against heuristic, level two-seat stalls. *The arms*: 30 screened (the relay, `FEED` either way, Phase 4 by travel turns, rival margin pads, relief and neutral pricing, target order, twelve constants); the margin splits by opponent. Fresh seeds: `FEED` 20 55.0%, the relay 50.5%; `FEED` 20 against the roster -1.1 / +1.0 / -1.5, not shipped. *What this leaves*: constants at optimum, what is left needs knowledge of the rival, the counter-punch not built.
- **Reading it.** Bots, not the posted maps (marshal is on most); read `beats` first; one |z| of 2 in 18 groups is chance; a rerun is the same run; the phase split is pooled raw turns; what a run costs.

## design/knower.md
- **Simultaneous resolution.** Why an oracle is possible at all.
- **What the oracle buys, and where.** A turn of warning, worth more the faster ships are. Depth 0 is not thinker.
- **Each Oracle setting against thinker and marshal.** Off / Predict / Search head to head in three cells. Only Search beats marshal; the ladder's 93% over thinker was a lucky 30 seeds.
- **Built, measured, removed.** Two ideas.
- **The depth search: branch the root, play the rest on.** Why branching deeper did nothing, and why more openings beat more turns. Most of the result against marshal comes from borrowing marshal.
- **Borrowed candidates (`EXTERNAL_CANDIDATES`).** Candidate win shares, contested decisions only, and why `SEARCH_WIDTH` is 2.
- **evolved as a borrowed candidate (2026-10-19).** `tools/candidate_bench.py`: evolved at Look-ahead Off, appended last for one seat, head to head against knower at the same stop (an exact null). Search +9.5 / +7.0 before `_flee`, +5.7 (z +3.68) after; Council +5.2, then +6.3 (z +3.49); one cell in 30 below zero. Picked on 6.4% of contested searches at Search, 12.7% at Council; what knower takes is its flow of ships to the front (58% of the garrison sent, twice the ships to its own systems). Against the roster at Search, with it and without, paired: +4.9 against marshal (z +3.50), +2.8 against actuary (z +1.51), three seats leaning worse. actuary in its place at Search: +13.4 (z +8.69). Cost on matched positions: evolved one more line (×1.11-1.22), actuary a tenth more (×1.23-1.34). evolved shipped 2026-10-20 at Council only (`COUNCIL_CANDIDATES`, after actuary, as measured); Search unchanged, actuary not borrowed there; Council's warning scaled by its lines (`COST_COUNCIL_LINES`); knower's bot-column rows stale; the traps unmoved.
- **How far to look.** The horizon is the longest lane plus `LANE_CUSHION`, which replaced a fixed depth, so Oracle is now Off / Predict / Search. Also the old depth curve.
- **Cost per decide, and where the search guard trips.** The `ply_ms` fit and `setup_warning`, which also counts rival bots that declare `decide_ms`. The browser is unmeasured; never measure on a loaded machine.
- **Seats it cannot run: stand-ins and people.** A predicting module's `stand_in_params` (actuary: Learning runs as Planned), run in the forecast and the rollouts and trusted. Against actuary-Learning, untrusted is null at Predict (z +0.95) and large at Search (z +9.50); trust adds z +6.54 / +3.88. People stay on `_blind`: a stronger bot is a worse forecast of them, and assuming they hold loses to bots flagged as people (z -3.33).

- **A doomed garrison leaves whole (2026-10-19).** A borrowed opening (heuristic's reserve) can leave ships on a system about to be overrun; `_flee` sends away a garrison certain to fall that would kill under half its number. Most dead in lopsided fights could not have left (new production, our own fleets landing); the fleeable rest is 0.65 ships a game at Search, 0.31 at Council. Search: 53.5% (z +3.4) then 51.2% (z +1.5) on fresh seeds, mostly from garrisons stepping into the attacker's emptied home; shipped on Search. Council: null on bots, 47.1% on the posted maps; not applied.
- **Pile-ups under the garrison-fights-last rule (2026-10-21).** `tools/pileup_bench.py`: rival arrivals folded in Phase 1 (defend) and a third seat's same-turn strike overpaid in `_required` (attack), for the seat under test only, on two posted 4- and 5-seat configs, line-ups all predicted exactly (*sole*) or two by stand-ins (*rivals*). Paired by *beats*, Δwon and Δturns: both null at Predict, Search and Council; the defensive fold changes 1-3% of threats, the attack fold about 1% of prices. Not built: the remnant strike, a person's launch folded as uncertain.
- **Baiting in a free-for-all (2026-10-21).** `tools/bait_bench.py`: Search offered openings that empty a wall between two rivals, on two posted 4- and 5-seat configs against a spread and a strong field. Picked in 1.3-2.2% of searches; pooled +2.25 pt (z +2.07), beats 52.8%, largest at five seats against the strong field (+5.5). A lean on screening seeds; confirmation on fresh seeds and Council not yet run.
